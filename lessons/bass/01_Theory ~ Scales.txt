### Theory In A Nutshell
Scales, triads, arpeggios, intervals and chords are the building blocks of music. Here’s how they fit together. Learn this page and the lingo inside out as it will make sense of a lot of more advanced ideas.
#### Scales
Think of scales as different colours or spices. All music uses them
and different scales have characteristic sounds. They are simply
collections of notes made up of a specific pattern. The most
common scales - major and natural minor - contain 7 different
notes.
IMAGE:bass_scales.png | Scales
#### Triads
'Tri' means three. A triad is made up of the 1st, 3rd and 5th notes of
a scale (that's 3 notes in total hence 'triad'). There are only 4
different types of triad and they form the basis of many bass lines.
IMAGE:bass_triads.png | Triads
#### Arpeggios
An arpeggio is the same as a triad except with the addition of the
eighth note in the scale (the 'octave'). You can play many cool lines
in lots of different styles just using triads and arpeggios.
IMAGE:bass_arpeggios.png | Arpeggios
#### Chords
If you play the notes of a triad together at the same time, rather
than one by one, you have a chord. The bass doesn't play chords
much in most styles of music but you can actually do a lot with
them; for example in solos, fills and making your practice more
interesting. Learning how to make chords from a scale, on the
bass or on the piano or guitar, will allow you to easily compose
your own music as well as understand how a lot of songs work.
IMAGE:bass_chords.png | Chords
#### Intervals
An interval is the distance between two musical pitches. All of the above are made up of intervals.
##### How Do You Use All Of This?
_Scales, triads, arpeggios and chords really are the building blocks of all bass lines. When you learn to play them you learn their sounds which in turn improves your ability to hear music. When you have a good ear and can recognise some of these sounds then you can work bass lines out more easily and also effortlessly create your own._
